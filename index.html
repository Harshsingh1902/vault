Act I  ·  The Lost Spark

There Was a World
Full of Noise

In a world that moved too fast and spoke too loud, there existed a traveler — neither searching for purpose nor fleeing from one. He simply moved, as travelers do.



And then, in the chaos of lecture halls and corridors and the relentless hum of college life — he saw her. A small, quiet child lost in the noise of the world. Not broken. Never broken. But unreached.



She carried within her a spark the world had not yet learned to see. It pulsed faintly — stubborn, patient, waiting for someone with the eyes to find it.

Act II  ·  The Guidance

The Traveler
Stepped In

He did not come to own her story. He did not come to be her destination. He came only to walk beside her — to hold a lantern where the road disappeared into shadow.



Like a certain beloved figure who crossed a border simply to return a child to where she belonged — the traveler's only mission was this: guide her safely through. Reunite her with her own strength. Her true home. And then, quietly, let go.



The bond that formed between them was never designed by logic. It grew in the margins — between shared silences and unspoken understanding, between the chaos of examinations and the stillness of just being known.

Euler's Identity — where entirely different worlds touch

There is a theorem in mathematics — one so strange and beautiful it makes seasoned mathematicians weep. It states that five utterly alien numbers, from five different mathematical universes, meet at a single, perfect point and dissolve into silence.



We were that equation.



Most of the time, I find myself unfit for conventional relationships — a solitary creature, by nature and by choice. But our alignment was a beautiful, rare anomaly against all mathematical odds. Two entirely different worlds, colliding perfectly for a fleeting moment, creating absolute peace.



Not forever. Not required to be. Perfect, precisely because it was finite.
